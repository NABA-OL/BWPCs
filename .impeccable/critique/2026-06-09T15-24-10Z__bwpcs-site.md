## Anti-Patterns Verdict

**Does this look AI-generated?**

**LLM assessment**: Mostly no — and that's genuine praise. The scroll-driven PC assembly animation is a real identity marker; it answers the AI-slop question by doing something specific to this brand. The Geist + JetBrains Mono pairing works because mono is doing real semantic work (prices, specs, codes), not just costuming. The weight-800/400 contrast hierarchy is committed and legible. The crimson accent discipline is solid.

Where it slips: the card grid template (`gap-px border bg-hairline` outer, `bg-vanilla p-6` inner) appears identically in three separate pages — home's "Cómo funciona", servicios' "Componentes", nosotros' "Valores". The identical structural grammar across the site's secondary surfaces, combined with the `PageHero` + sections + CTA-strip formula repeated on every page, creates a monotony that counteracts the animation-driven home page.

**Deterministic scan**: 1 finding.
- `layout-transition` warning in `src/app/globals.css:96` — `transition: max-width` on `.bw-grow` (the wordmark expand class). Animating `max-width` causes layout recalculation on every frame, producing jank during the wordmark reveal. Fix: use `scaleX` transform with `transform-origin: left`, or the `grid-template-columns: 0fr → 1fr` trick.

No false positives. The detector didn't flag gradient text, eyebrow patterns, or hero-metric templates (none are present).

**Visual overlays**: browser automation not available for this run; no overlay output.

## Overall Impression

The home page is the strongest surface on the site — the assembly animation earns the brand, the hero typography is confident, and the asymmetric "Lo que hacemos" split breaks the grid well. The problem is that everything after the home page feels like a photocopy of the same template: hero + grid cards + CTA strip, repeated. The site has a strong identity moment and then stops investing in it.

---

## What's Working

**1. The scroll-driven PC assembly.** This is the site's single biggest differentiator. A line-art PC assembling itself as you scroll past the hero — specific, technical, unmistakably this brand. Most sites in this category (local PC builder, Colombia, budget-friendly) would have a stock photo of a gaming setup. B&W has a custom animation. Keep it, protect it.

**2. Price typography.** The cotizador's dark panel with `font-mono text-4xl` total in vanilla-on-panda is excellent information design. The total number commands the panel; the sub-totals (tarjeta/PSE) recede into secondary role with `text-vanilla/60`. JetBrains Mono makes prices feel computed and precise rather than estimated. This is the right hierarchy for a tool that builds user trust through accuracy.

**3. Typographic restraint.** Single family (Geist), weight contrast as the only hierarchy tool, no decorative color leaking into headings. The crimson appears only where it must — active nav state, the "Principal" label, the CTA button. Its rarity makes it work.

## Priority Issues

**[P1] CTA button hover state is near-invisible in context**
- **What**: Section CTA buttons (`bg-accent text-accent-ink hover:bg-vanilla hover:text-panda`) hover to a vanilla background. On the `bg-component/30` section strip (the faint gray CTA band), hovering the crimson button turns it vanilla-on-near-vanilla — barely distinguishable from the surrounding surface.
- **Why it matters**: The CTA button is the primary conversion action on every page. A broken hover state undermines the signal precisely when the user is deciding to act.
- **Fix**: Change hover to `hover:bg-accent-deep` (the darker crimson) to keep the button clearly visible on all backgrounds. Reserve vanilla-background hover for cases where the button sits on a panda background.
- **Command**: `/impeccable polish`

**[P1] Identical card grid template on every surface**
- **What**: The `gap-px overflow-hidden border border-hairline bg-hairline` + `bg-vanilla p[4-6]` cell pattern appears verbatim on: home "Cómo funciona" (numbered), servicios "Componentes" (4-col), servicios "Cómo funciona" (3-col numbered), nosotros "Valores" (3-col). Every secondary content section maps to the same template.
- **Why it matters**: This is the "identical card grid" absolute ban in practice. Each page visit feels like a shuffle of the same content into the same box. Users who visit more than one page notice the template before they read the content. The strong home page is followed by three pages that look like they were built from a single starter component.
- **Fix**: Differentiate the display pattern per page. The Servicios components could be a definition list or a dense two-column table. The Nosotros valores could be a horizontal rule + prose layout. The process steps could be a vertical timeline. At minimum, vary padding, borders, and background tinting enough that the templates feel distinct.
- **Command**: `/impeccable layout`

**[P1] Native `<select>` breaks visual consistency on mobile**
- **What**: The cotizador uses native `<select>` for component tier selection. On Android (the primary audience — WhatsApp mobile traffic), native selects render with the system's own chrome: rounded background, system font, bottom-sheet picker. On iOS, they open a wheel picker. Neither matches the sharp-border, Geist-mono visual language.
- **Why it matters**: The cotizador is the highest-stakes UI on the site — it's the moment the customer decides to contact. A jarring system-native control at exactly that point breaks the premium-local-expert perception the brand is trying to project.
- **Fix**: Replace with a custom `<select>`-compatible component (styled dropdown with `appearance-none` + custom chevron) or a segmented button group for 3-4 options. The existing border and bg-vanilla system already provides the right visual vocabulary — just suppress the system chrome and add a downward chevron.
- **Command**: `/impeccable polish`

**[P2] Max-width layout animation (detector confirmed)**
- **What**: `.bw-grow` uses `transition: max-width 1s ease` for the wordmark expand. The detector flagged this at `globals.css:96`. `max-width` triggers full layout recalculation on every frame.
- **Why it matters**: The wordmark animation plays on every page load in the header. On mid-range Androids (the primary audience), this produces visible jank during the first second of every page visit.
- **Fix**: Replace with `display: grid; grid-template-columns: 0fr` → `1fr` on the parent, or use `scaleX(0)` → `scaleX(1)` with `transform-origin: left`. Either preserves the visual effect without touching layout on every frame.
- **Command**: `/impeccable polish`

**[P2] PageHero eyebrow repeats as page-level grammar**
- **What**: Every secondary page opens with the same small `font-mono text-sm text-component` eyebrow above the h1: "Lo que ofrecemos," "Sobre B & W," "Hablemos," "Estimado aproximado." The labels are not uppercase-tracked, which avoids the most flagrant version of the pattern, but the identical component placement on every page makes it read as scaffolding rather than voice.
- **Why it matters**: The first thing users see on any inner page is the same visual grammar. After the home page (which has no eyebrow), the repetition flattens the experience and makes all pages feel interchangeable.
- **Fix**: Not every page needs an eyebrow. The contacto page's "Hablemos" eyebrow is redundant — the h1 "Contacto" is already clear. Keep eyebrows only where they genuinely add context (the cotizador's "Estimado aproximado" earns it because it sets expectations). Remove or replace the others with a different entry device (a larger h1 variant, a decorative divider, or nothing).
- **Command**: `/impeccable distill`

## Persona Red Flags

**Jordan (First-Timer) — Colombian user who heard about B&W on WhatsApp but has never bought PC components**

Jordan lands on the cotizador from a WhatsApp link. She sees a list of categories with checkboxes and selects. The `<select>` options say things like "Gama media · $450,000." She doesn't know what "gama media" means relative to her needs. There's no tooltip, no tooltip-trigger, no comparison table. She checks everything on the list (because more must be better), inflating her estimate. The total shows $3,200,000. She has no idea if that's reasonable. The disclaimer says "precio aproximado, sujeto a cambios" — which she reads as "this number is meaningless." She closes the tab instead of writing.

Red flags:
- "Gama" is unexplained. "Gama baja," "media," "alta" need a one-line contextual hint ("for everyday tasks," "for gaming," "for content creation").
- No price anchoring. The total has no "typical build" reference point. Is $3M reasonable or wild?
- The disclaimer reads as a warning, not a reassurance. Reword it.
- No visible "ask us" CTA inside the cotizador tool itself (only the WhatsApp button after the estimate, not alongside the configuration).

**Casey (Distracted Mobile) — User browsing the site one-handed on an Android phone while commuting**

Casey opens the cotizador. The component select dropdowns require accurate tapping on a `min-w-[200px]` element on a 375px screen — that leaves 175px for tap precision. The native select opens a full-screen picker that dismisses her scroll position. She gets through three components, gets interrupted by a call, comes back — the page has reloaded (Next.js ISR) and her selections are gone. She starts over, rushes, sends the WhatsApp message without reading it. The auto-generated message contains "Fuente: gama baja" which she didn't intend.

Red flags:
- No state persistence across interruptions. Cotizador selections are lost on page reload.
- Native select picker on Android is a full-screen takeover — heavy context switch for a lightweight interaction.
- No undo or "go back to defaults" button.
- The WhatsApp CTA is at the bottom of the sticky panel — on mobile, the panel is not sticky (it falls below the selection list). The CTA is below the fold.

**Miguel (The PC Enthusiast) — Knows exactly what he wants: Ryzen 7, RTX 4070, DDR5. Comes to B&W for pricing.**

Miguel hits the site looking for specific model availability and USD-based pricing. The cotizador gives him gama labels, not model names. He can't see whether "gama alta · $2,800,000" means an RTX 4070 or an RTX 4060 Ti. He needs to write to WhatsApp anyway. He's fine with WhatsApp, but the pre-generated message says "Gráfica: gama alta" — Miguel needs to overwrite this manually with his actual target. The cotizador hasn't saved him any time.

Red flags:
- No model information visible in tier labels. "Gama alta" is too vague for enthusiasts.
- No direct link to a component catalog or price list.
- The cotizador is positioned as the pre-contact step, but enthusiasts arrive with specs, not with gama preferences.

## Minor Observations

- The mobile menu has no enter/exit transition. It appears and disappears instantly, which is jarring. A 150ms height or opacity transition would fix this.
- When the mobile menu is open, focus is not moved into the menu. Screen reader users tabbing through the page will tab past the burger into the body content behind the menu.
- `text-panda/70` (≈70% black on vanilla) is used as body text in the home CTA section. This falls below 4.5:1 contrast ratio. Switching to full `text-panda` or `text-panda/80` would bring it above threshold without visible change.
- The footer logo uses `className="invert"` on a PNG. If the logo PNG has any non-binary pixels (anti-aliasing on the panda illustration), `invert` can produce an off-white halo instead of clean white. Worth verifying at high DPI.
- The home page's "Cómo funciona" numbered steps use `text-5xl font-extrabold text-hairline` — the numbers are #E5E5E5 (hairline) on vanilla (#FAF8F3). Contrast ratio ≈ 1.1:1, essentially invisible. This is intentional (avoiding the numbered-scaffolding pattern) but the numbers provide zero value as rendered. Either make them slightly darker or remove them.
- CLAUDE.md still references "azul eléctrico #2563EB" as the accent, but the actual code uses `#c83c2a` (Warm Crimson). This documentation drift could mislead future contributors.

## Questions to Consider

- The cotizador's primary job is to get users to WhatsApp. But users who know their specs bypass gama labels. What if the cotizador had two paths: "I don't know what I need" (current flow) and "I know my specs" (free-text or spec entry)?
- Every secondary page ends in the same CTA strip. Is that section earning its place on every page, or could some pages end by letting the content breathe — with a quieter next step?
- The fixed PC backdrop is a strong identity element on the home page, but on the cotizador it visually competes with the interactive form. Should the cotizador suppress the backdrop or fade it further?
